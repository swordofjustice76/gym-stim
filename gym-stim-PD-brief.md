1. THE CORE IDEA

GYM STIM is a premium, reloadable aluminium gym/EDC stick designed to consolidate things a serious gym user might otherwise carry separately.

The core Elite concept contains:

1. Smelling salts
2. Electrolyte chews
3. The user's own pre-workout powder

The user already owns their pre-workout tub and water bottle.

The product does NOT need to force the user into buying GYM STIM's pre-workout.

Core proposition:

«Three training essentials. One pocket object.»

The stick should feel like a premium piece of gym equipment / EDC rather than a cheap supplement container.

Think:

- Premium tactical flashlight
- High-end EDC tool
- Machined aluminium object
- Mechanical satisfaction
- Pocket clip
- Heavy, durable, cold-metal feel

It should look like something a serious lifter would actually WANT to carry.

2. CURRENT LOCKED ELITE ARCHITECTURE

The current baseline specification is:

Body

- 6061-T6 aluminium
- Approximately 38 mm diameter
- Approximately 190 mm long
- Type II anodised
- Pocket clip
- Loaded target weight approximately 140–180 g
- Fat-flashlight proportions rather than a thin vape/pen

The 38 × 190 mm dimensions exist because the product needs a real powder flask. Do NOT arbitrarily make it slimmer just because slim looks attractive.

3. THREE-CHAMBER SYSTEM

CHAMBER A — SNAP / SMELLING SALTS

At the top.

Current concept:

- Twist cap
- Approximately 3–4 ml well
- Smelling salt + menthol
- Dry/unactivated for travel
- Factory refill caps only
- User does NOT homebrew ammonia
- Warning/poison marking on the cap

The salt component is deliberately modular.

Potential mechanisms to investigate:

A1 — Twist-to-open cap

Rotate 90–180 degrees to expose the opening.

A2 — Flip aperture

Small hinged opening operated by thumb.

A3 — Rotating turret

Outer ring rotates to reveal an inhalation port.

A4 — Pull + twist safety mechanism

Two-stage opening to prevent accidental activation inside a gym bag.

A5 — Replaceable salt cartridge/cap

The entire salt section screws onto the body and can be replaced.

Investigate all five visually and mechanically.

The replaceable cartridge/cap is particularly interesting because it keeps the aluminium body reusable while allowing the regulated consumable to remain a separate component.

---

4. CHAMBER B — ELECTROLYTE CHEWS

Current baseline:

- Replaceable magazine
- Approximately 10 chews
- Target chew geometry approximately 16 mm × 5–6 mm
- Approximately 55 mm stack
- Approximately 18 mm bore
- One-handed operation
- Caffeine-free chew
- Optional: the product works with this chamber empty

Do NOT put caffeine in both the chew and the pre-workout at launch.

The chew mechanism is an area where I want serious experimentation.

Mechanism concepts

B1 — Gravity stack

Chews vertically stacked and released through gravity.

B2 — Spring-loaded magazine

A spring continuously pushes the stack toward the dispensing position.

B3 — Push-button magazine

Press a button and exactly one chew advances.

B4 — Sliding dispenser

A thumb slider moves one chew into a pickup position.

B5 — Rotary carousel

Ten chews sit around a rotating wheel. Rotate to select/dispense.

B6 — Removable cassette

The complete 10-chew magazine slides into the aluminium body and locks into place.

The cassette could potentially be a low-cost food-contact plastic component while the external body remains premium aluminium.

Important

I want these mechanisms compared for:

- Reliability
- Ease of manufacture
- Number of parts
- Hygiene
- Moisture resistance
- Accidental dispensing
- One-handed operation
- User satisfaction
- “Premium mechanical” feeling
- Cleaning
- Replacement cost

The final mechanism should feel satisfying.

A good benchmark is the tactile feeling of:

«CLICK → dispense → CLICK»

rather than something that feels like a pill container.

5. CHAMBER C — POWDER FLASK

This is the most important engineering challenge.

The user fills the flask at home from their own pre-workout tub.

Current baseline:

- Approximately 70 ml capacity
- Wide-mouth opening
- Screw-off flask
- Fill markings at approximately 20 / 45 / 70 ml
- Desiccant in lid
- Funnel included
- User pours/dumps powder into their existing water bottle

The flask should be designed around volume, NOT the assumption that every pre-workout has the same grams-per-scoop.

Different products have different serving sizes and powder densities.

Therefore:

«70 ml means 70 ml capacity, not “three servings.”»

Do not make unsupported claims such as “one flask always equals X scoops.”

---

6. POWDER FLASK CONCEPTS TO EXPLORE

C1 — Simple removable flask

Current baseline.

Unscrew → fill → screw back into stick → remove at gym → pour.

This is likely the easiest to manufacture.

C2 — Integrated scoop

A small scoop is integrated into the flask lid or stored within the flask.

Goal:

«No separate scoop required.»

C3 — Funnel-neck flask

The flask has a wide filling opening but tapers into a controlled pouring neck.

Goal:

- Easy filling
- Minimal powder waste
- Easy transfer into water bottle

C4 — Bottom trapdoor

The flask remains attached to the stick.

Press a release and a sealed bottom hatch opens, allowing powder to fall directly into the water bottle.

Investigate this despite the increased mechanical complexity.

C5 — Twist-to-pour

The flask rotates or unlocks into a controlled pouring position.

C6 — Side-loading flask

The flask slides sideways out of the body like a magazine.

This could make the product visually distinctive.

---

7. POWDER FLASK SIZES

Investigate:

40 ml

For concentrated/light-serving pre-workouts.

70 ml

Current preferred baseline.

100 ml

For large serving-size powders.

Do NOT automatically recommend launching all three.

Prototype/testing should determine whether one size is sufficient.

The key question is:

«What percentage of common pre-workout products can realistically be carried in each size?»

The user should never need to portion powder into multiple tiny cells.

One wide-mouth flask is preferred.

15. LAUNCH ARCHITECTURE

The Australian launch structure is intentionally conservative.

The initial product is an:

«EMPTY HARDWARE DEVICE»

Launch box:

- Empty Elite body
- Empty powder flask
- Funnel
- Desiccant puck
- Wipe card
- Fill-from-your-own-tub instructions

Do NOT combine charged smelling salts, food and hardware into a single launch multipack without appropriate legal review.

Later consumables remain separate SKUs with their own packaging/labels.

The hardware should function perfectly well without branded GYM STIM consumables.

This is a critical strategic principle.

18. ENGINEERING QUESTIONS

For every concept, identify:

- Approximate dimensions
- Internal volume
- Number of components
- Materials
- Thread requirements
- O-rings/seals
- Tolerances likely to matter
- Food-contact materials
- Moisture risks
- Powder contamination risks
- Cleaning requirements
- Manufacturing process
- CNC requirements
- Injection-moulded components
- Assembly requirements
- Likely failure points
- Likely manufacturing cost range
- Prototype difficulty
- Mass-production difficulty

Do not pretend exact costs or tolerances are known when they aren't.

Clearly label:

- Known
- Estimated
- Assumption
- Requires prototype testing

---

19. CRITICAL DESIGN CONSTRAINTS

Do NOT resurrect previously rejected ideas.

Do NOT:

- Turn the stick into a vape
- Inhale powder
- Make a citrulline pen
- Make an open powder hopper
- Use four tiny powder cells
- Put ammonia into the drink
- Put ammonia into a plastic “Daily Elite”
- Put caffeine in both chew and Flood at launch
- Make branded Flood mandatory
- Use a puck-only powder system unless the powder flask genuinely fails
- Make performance/medical/1RM claims
- Add electronics without a compelling reason
- Sacrifice functionality purely to make it thinner

The powder flask remains the preferred solution.
